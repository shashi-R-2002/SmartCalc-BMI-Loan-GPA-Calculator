    About BMI

    BMI (Body Mass Index) is a simple measure that uses your height and weight to estimate your body fat. It helps determine whether you are underweight, at a healthy weight, overweight, or obese.

    BMI Formula
    BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height² (m) (Height measured in meters)

    BMI Categories

    BMI RangeCategory
    Below 18.5Underweight
    18.5 – 24.9Normal weight
    25 – 29.9Overweight
    30 – 34.9Obese
    35 and aboveExtremely obese

    Why BMI Matters

    • Helps assess your weight status
    • Can indicate potential health risks
    • Useful for general health screening
    • Easy and quick to calculate
    • Helps you set realistic health goals

    Important: BMI is a general guideline and not a medical diagnosis. Always consult a healthcare professional for personalized advice.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is BMI accurate for athletes?

    Not always — muscle is denser than fat, so muscular people can register as "overweight" despite having low body fat.

    Does BMI differ for children and teens?

    Yes. Kids and teens are measured against age- and sex-specific growth charts rather than the adult scale used here.

    What should I do with a high or low result?

    Treat it as a prompt to check in with a healthcare provider, who can look at your full health picture rather than one number.

    How often should I check my BMI?

    Every few months is plenty for most people — weight naturally fluctuates day to day, so frequent checks rarely tell you much new.

    About This Calculator

    Estimates the fixed monthly payment on a standard amortising loan — the kind used for personal loans, auto loans, and many mortgages.

    Payment Formula
    M = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ [(1+r)ⁿ − 1] P = principal, r = monthly rate, n = number of payments

    How It Works

    Your annual rate is converted to a monthly rate, and the term to a number of monthly payments. The loan is spread evenly so every instalment is the same size — early payments carry more interest, later ones more principal.

    Extra payments reduce your balance faster, lowering total interest paid over the life of the loan.

    Good To Know

    • Compare rates across lenders
    • Check for origination fees
    • Ask if the rate is fixed or variable
    • Factor in insurance requirements

    Important: Figures are estimates only. Confirm exact terms with your lender before borrowing.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why is my first payment mostly interest?

    Interest is charged on the remaining balance, which is largest at the start of the loan, so early payments carry more interest and less principal.

    Does paying extra reduce my total interest?

    Yes — extra payments shrink the principal faster, which lowers the interest charged on every payment that follows.

    What if my rate is variable, not fixed?

    This calculator assumes a fixed rate for the whole term. A variable rate will change your actual payments as market rates move.

    Should I compare offers from multiple lenders?

    Yes — rates, fees, and terms can vary a lot between lenders for the same loan amount, so it's worth shopping around before committing.

    About GPA

    Grade Point Average summarises your performance across subjects into a single number, weighted by how many credits each subject is worth.

    GPA Formula
    GPA = Σ(Grade Point × Credit) ÷ Σ(Credits)

    Grade Point Reference

    Tips

    • Higher-credit courses move your GPA more
    • Exclude pass/fail courses if your school does
    • Recalculate each term to track trends

    Note: Grading scales differ between institutions — match the points to your school's scale.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    My school uses a different grading scale — can I still use this?

    The formula stays the same, but check your institution's official grade-point table and pick the closest matching grade for each subject.

    Should I include pass/fail subjects?

    Most institutions exclude pass/fail courses from GPA. Leave those out unless your school specifically says to include them.

    Can I remove a subject after adding it?

    Yes — use the × button next to any subject row to remove it before recalculating your GPA.

    Does a low grade in a low-credit course hurt my GPA much?

    Less than a low grade in a high-credit course — credits act as weights, so higher-credit subjects move your overall GPA more.
